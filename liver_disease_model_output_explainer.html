Why did Logistic Regression beat XGBoost?
The test set is small (117 patients). LR achieved 0.826 AUC vs XGBoost's 0.809 — this gap could easily reverse with more data or a different random split. Don't over-read the ranking.
Optuna boosted XGBoost from 0.881 → 0.908 AUC (CV)
The tuned XGBoost's best CV score was 0.908 — but its test AUC dropped to 0.809. This is mild overfitting to the CV folds. Add more regularisation (higher reg_alpha).
Stacking didn't outperform base models
Stacking (0.805 AUC) tied XGBoost Tuned. With only 117 test samples, stacking's advantage needs more data to show. This is expected on small datasets.
26 disease patients were missed (false negatives)
LR's recall is 68.7% — meaning 1 in 3 sick patients is missed. In a clinical setting, lower your threshold from 0.5 to ~0.35 to prioritise recall over precision.
LightGBM has the best F1 (0.845) on test
LightGBM achieves the highest F1 score and best accuracy (77.8%). For balanced precision/recall, it's actually the strongest model despite ranking lower by AUC.
SMOTE worked — class-wise results improved
Healthy-class recall reached 88.2% (specificity), meaning the model correctly identifies most truly healthy patients too — not just biased toward the majority class.
